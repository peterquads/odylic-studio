The "weird C" is just an A minor that moved house

Ivy's signature chord sounds strange because it isn't a normal C. Fret an A minor, then drop your pinky onto the 3rd fret of the A string. The top four strings never move — only the bass note changes. That's a C6, and it's why the song shimmers.

The loop

This four-bar cycle carries the intro and the chorus. One bar each, straight through, over and over. Notice bars 2 and 4 are the same chord — the movement you hear is the C6 falling to Am, not the D minor.

The verse walks down

The second section keeps a similar shape on top while the bass note descends: G under an E minor, then F under a D minor. Same picking pattern, same right hand — you're only changing what your thumb lands on.

Em/G → Dm/F

Play Em with your ring finger on the 3rd fret of the low E string, then slide the whole idea to a D minor with an F in the bass. The top strings stay ringing throughout.

Why it sounds like that

The song sits in D dorian — a D minor scale with a raised 6th. That's where the E minor and the B diminished come from, and why it never quite settles into sadness.

Tone

Chorus, generously

The recorded guitar is drenched in it. That watery, slightly detuned wobble is doing half the work of the arrangement.

Reverb, then more

A big hall setting, clean amp, neck pickup. On acoustic, play softly with the flesh of your fingertips rather than nails.

Let it ring

Never damp between chords. The overlap of one voicing bleeding into the next is the entire character of the track.
